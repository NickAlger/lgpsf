**How the work was driven.** Nick repeatedly pushed back on assertions
that hadn't been measured, and it paid off every time:

- "By zero, do you mean numerically small, or exactly zero?" -> a
  forensic investigation showing the table's zeros are exact and
  structural (parity classes + Gram-Schmidt staircase), not a tolerance
  question. That turned a risky-looking change into a safe one.
- "Reason from first principles about how this should be structured" ->
  the harmonic_polynomials module split, which the mode-set factorization
  and everything after it depended on.
- "Will this break the other Hessian?" -> caught that contracting the
  gradient inside the LG layer would have quietly foreclosed the exact
  Golub-Pereyra reduced Gauss-Newton Hessian. The uncontracted
  `grad_lg_basis` exists because of that question.
- Two of MY claims were falsified by measurement I only ran because the
  discipline was established: the shell regrouping is NOT more accurate
  (a wash at ~1.5 ulp), and its end-to-end Python win is 1.02x, not the
  1.5-2.4x the microbenchmark suggested.

**Standing lesson for the port** (also in the plan): Python timings do
not predict C++ relative performance here. ~80% of the prototype's LG
evaluation time is numpy per-call dispatch overhead, measured by scaling
the point count and reading off the intercept. Re-measure on the C++
side; do not port a performance conclusion.

**Bit-identity as the acceptance criterion** caught a 1-ULP regression
that `allclose` would have passed (a rewrite multiplied `genlaguerre`
and the Gaussian separately where the original grouped them into a
`radial` intermediate first). Use it for any change that is supposed to
be a pure restructuring; use a tolerance only where the math genuinely
reassociates, and say so.

### The shape the operator layer settled into

`lg_operator.hpp` defines `LGOperator` and every evaluation/assembly helper,
and depends on NONE of the fitting stack. `operator_fit.hpp` is one producer of
that structure. `test_lg_operator.cpp` never includes `operator_fit.hpp` and
builds every operator by hand, so the independence is a build property rather
than a claim -- if a fitting dependency creeps back in, that file stops
compiling.

Two conventions worth not re-deriving:

- Methods for "what am I" (`num_rows`, `has_model`, `row_window`, `row_modes`);
  free functions for "what can be done with me". The type is a transparent
  flat-array structure meant to be inspected, MPI-gathered, merged and
  serialized, so private fields would fight the point of it.
- One truncation rule everywhere: `support = window ∩ fitted tau-ellipsoid`,
  `truncation_tau` defaulting to infinity, `eval_kernel_unrestricted` the named
  opt-out.

**2. Probe-budget sweep, and the counting-rule fix is worth 16% at k=40.**

| k | proto +sym | C++ +sym | proto time | C++ time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.0608 | 0.0603 | 471.7 s (6 proc) | 42.5 s (4 thr) |
| 40 | 0.0320 | **0.0269** | 1315.9 s | 185.3 s |
| 100 | 0.0147 | 0.0147 | 3478.7 s | 785.9 s |

Fully explained by the BASELINE GUARD's admissibility. The baseline is a
linear fit at a pinned theta; the prototype counted the free-mu parameter size
(5 in 2D), the C++ counts the pinned size (3), so the cap on the baseline's
mode count moves: 3 -> 6 at k=20, 10 -> 15 at k=40, 28 -> 28 at k=100. Where
the caps coincide (k=100) the two agree to four digits on both variants; where
the gap is widest (k=40) the C++ ships a 15-mode a-priori baseline on 617 rows
instead of a 10-mode one on 192, and the operator improves 16%. The searched
fit winning LESS often is what helped -- windowed-CV optimism, caught by a
stronger status quo.

### Performance profile (2026-07-26, cont.)

Smooth-beta PIG, ellipsoid window, WedgeLadder(10,2), k = 20/40/100, callgrind.
Full record in `dev/perf-2026-07-26.md`; harness `dev/prof_pig.cpp`,
solver microbenchmark `dev/bench_solve.cpp`.

**The headline: the fit is SVD-bound, not basis-bound.** 23.9% / 49.6% / 60.9%
of all instructions at k=20/40/100 is the SVD. The earlier timing note's "the
LM is essentially all basis evaluation" was measured at 6 modes and does not
survive production mode counts (4.0 / 12.1 / 21.2 modes per row here).

**It is replaceable exactly**: `inner_solve`'s `sigma/(sigma^2+ridge)` filter IS
Tikhonov, so a thin QR plus an m x m Cholesky computes the same `c`, and the QR's
Q is the orthonormal range basis the Kaufman Jacobian wants. Prototyped on
patched headers (`LGPSF_INNER_SOLVE_QR`, in the scratchpad, not the repo):

- 1.15x / 1.41x / 1.57x per row at k=20/40/100
- whole-field shells-L6 k=40: 185.3 -> 117.6 s ball, 140.8 -> 95.0 s ellipsoid
- **field error identical to four digits** (0.0354 / 0.0269 either way); 3 rows
  of 6557 change their searched/baseline decision
- test suite 124/126 cases, and the 2 failures are exactly the predicted ones:
  the rank-deficiency test and the Golub-Pereyra Jacobian (needs sigma and V)

A landable version keeps the SVD as a fallback for those two cases and uses
ColPivHouseholderQR otherwise -- ~30 lines in one function. NOT implemented;
Nick's call, since the bar for changing working code is high.

**Checked and found not worth it**: allocator churn (~21% of instructions at
k=20, but fixing it means the evaluation objects carry reusable buffers, which
breaks the layer's confinement); Eigen re-packing the fixed `z_hat` on every

### M5 slice 5b: PIG replay through the bindings (2026-07-27)

`dev/pig_bindings_replay.py` reads the exact bytes `dev/pig_fit.cpp` reads,
runs the same configuration through `lgpsf.fit_operator`, and compares
score / baseline_score / status / all three deployment variants.

**Result: BIT-IDENTICAL, both window shapes, every check** -- once the
reference is compiled with the same flags as the bindings module.

It was NOT identical at first, and the diagnosis is worth keeping. The bridge
was built `-march=native`, the bindings module plain `-O3`. Disagreement over
the 5076 live rows: median |dscore| 1.1e-16 (one ULP), 91% differing at all,
but only **4 rows (0.08%) by more than 1e-3** -- max relative 0.62 on one row.
`status` was identical even then, so no row changed its ship decision.

That tail is the problem's fault, not the marshalling's: `-march=native`
reorders floating-point reductions, and a 1-ULP difference is occasionally
enough to send the LM loop into a different local minimum of a multimodal
VarPro landscape. Recorded in `docs/design-notes.md`, and the M5 acceptance
criterion is now qualified -- bit-identity is a within-build property, not a
cross-build one.

floating point; 5% differing by 1e-3 would not be.

like with like. Rebuild it with `-march=native` before doing timing work.

### Follow-up: which rows are -march sensitive, and does it matter (2026-07-27)

Nick asked for detail rather than a fix. Built a 2x2 of binding modules --
{QR, SVD} x {plain -O3, -march=native} -- and compared row by row against the
held-out pool. Scratch builds left in place: `build-py-native`,
`build-py-svd`, `build-py-svd-native` (the SVD ones compile against a patched
header copy in the session scratchpad that forces `InnerFactors::Full`).
Probe script `dev/pig_march_probe.py`.

Findings, all recorded in `docs/design-notes.md`:

- **Not the QR's doing.** At matched flags QR and SVD agree to one ULP with
  zero rows differing >1e-3 and the same global metric to 8 digits. Under
  `-march=native` the SVD flips the SAME four rows. The reduction order is the
  cause; the inner solve is irrelevant.
- **The rows are knife-edge rows at the ice margin**: 3 of 4 sit 3-7 km from a
  dead node against a field median of 85 km. NOT small -- one carries 6x the
  median row energy. Two are in the worst 10%/3% of the field by fit quality,
  which is the mechanism: a hard row has a flat or multimodal objective, and
  that is where one ULP picks the basin. Two of four changed MODE-LADDER RUNG
  (3 vs 6 modes), so the flip is discrete.
- **No consequence.** Mean held-out qc identical (0.1702), native better on
  45% of rows, global error 0.05209035 vs 0.05208739.

No action proposed. Recorded because "a handful of rows differ" is the kind of
observation that costs a day if someone meets it cold.
