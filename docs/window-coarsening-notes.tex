\title{Graded window coarsening: a per-row work bound for the\\
       operator fit that needs no estimate of the kernel's width}
\author{Nick Alger with Claude Fable 5.1}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A row of the LG-PSF operator is fitted on its \emph{window}, every column
dof inside $\tau_w$ times the caller's a-priori ellipsoid, and the fit's cost
is linear in the window's point count. The window is only as good as the
prior: on the maintainer's ice-sheet Hessian at continental scale a few
hundred rows whose prior was too wide by decades had windows of about
$90{,}000$ points, sat on one rank, and that rank's fit took $5{,}900$\,s
against a mean of $75$\,s. This note records the fix. The fit's design
matrix is a mass-weighted quadrature of the model's action on the probes, so
subsampling the window is choosing a coarser quadrature, and one quadrature
-- cells graded by distance from the row's centre, size at most $\eps$ times
that distance, floored at single points -- is adequate for every kernel width
at once. Each cell becomes its mass-weighted centroid, its summed mass and
the mass-weighted mean of every probe field: the in-cell probe sum is kept
exactly, only the basis function's variation over the cell is approximated.
The deployed support is the full window; only the fit's quadrature on it is
coarsened, with controlled error, and the two finalists are re-scored on the
full window before the baseline guard decides. Measured on sixty real rows
with windows of 250 to $100{,}000$ points, $\eps = 0.1$ keeps every guard
decision and 97\% of ladder levels for 11--54$\times$ less wall time on the
largest windows. Whitening is as in \code{varpro-whitening-notes.pdf}; the
measurements are \code{experiments/window-coarsening.md}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The fit is a quadrature}

Fix a row $\rho$ with mass $m_\rho$ and its window of $K$ column points $x_j$
with masses $m_j = (\Mtwo)_{jj}$. Probes $z_q$, $q = 1,\dots,k$, are
restricted to the window and the data are $y_q = (Hz_q)(\rho)$. (The
whitening note indexes probes by $\ell$; here $\ell$ is reserved for the
angular order of a mode.) After noise whitening the row fit is the ordinary
least-squares problem
\begin{equation}
\what y_q \;\approx\; \sum_i c_i\, A_{qi}(\theta) \;+\; s\, B_q,
\qquad
A_{qi}(\theta) = \sqrt{m_\rho}\sum_{j=1}^{K} m_j\, z_{jq}\, \phi_i(x_j;\theta),
\qquad
B_q = \sqrt{m_\rho}\; z_{\mathrm{spike},q},
\label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
with $\what y_q = y_q/\sqrt{m_\rho}$, $\phi_i(\cdot\,;\theta)$ the
Laguerre--Gaussian modes in the ellipsoid parametrized by $\theta$, and $s$
the spike coefficient. The unknowns $(\theta, c, s)$ are fitted from $k$
equations indexed by the probes. The window enters only through the sum over
$j$ in $A_{qi}$, a mass-weighted quadrature of $\int z_q(x)\,\phi_i(x;\theta)
\,dx$ over the window; the points are never regressed on. Two things follow.
The cost of the fit is linear in $K$ (every basis evaluation is over $K$
points, and basis evaluation is about half of a row fit), and ``subsample
the window'' means ``choose a coarser quadrature of the model's action on the
probes''. A coarser quadrature is then judged as a quadrature: by the error
in the columns $A_{qi}$ at the $\theta$ the search visits.

\section{Aggregation: what stays exact}

Partition the window into cells $C$ and replace each by one quadrature point,
\begin{equation}
m_C = \sum_{j\in C} m_j, \qquad
x_C = \frac{1}{m_C}\sum_{j\in C} m_j\, x_j, \qquad
z_{Cq} = \frac{1}{m_C}\sum_{j\in C} m_j\, z_{jq},
\label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
so that the coarse column is
\begin{equation}
\tilde A_{qi}(\theta) = \sqrt{m_\rho}\sum_C m_C\, z_{Cq}\, \phi_i(x_C;\theta)
 = \sqrt{m_\rho}\sum_C \Big(\sum_{j\in C} m_j z_{jq}\Big)\, \phi_i(x_C;\theta).
\label{eq:coarse-design}
\end{equation}
The mass and the probe sum of every cell are preserved, $\sum_C m_C = \sum_j
m_j$ and $\sum_C m_C z_{Cq} = \sum_j m_j z_{jq}$ (to $10^{-14}$ relative in
every test). What is approximated is the basis function's variation over the
cell, by the midpoint rule at the centroid. Expanding $\phi_i$ about $x_C$,
one cell's error is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j\in C} m_j z_{jq}\,\phi_i(x_j) - \Big(\sum_{j\in C} m_j z_{jq}\Big)\phi_i(x_C)
 = \nabla\phi_i(x_C)\cdot d_{Cq} + O(h_C^2), \qquad
d_{Cq} = \sum_{j\in C} m_j z_{jq}\,(x_j - x_C),
\label{eq:dipole}
\end{equation}
with $h_C$ the cell's size and $d_{Cq}$ the probe's \emph{dipole} inside the
cell. The centroid makes the in-cell first moment of the masses vanish,
$\sum_{j\in C} m_j (x_j - x_C) = 0$; so for a probe that is smooth over the
cell, $z_{jq} = z_{Cq} + O(h_C)$, the dipole is $O(h_C^2)$ and the error is
second order. For a white-noise probe, the
default, the dipole is as large as the monopole and the error is \emph{first}
order in $h_C$ -- but zero-mean and independent across probes, so it enters
the regression like noise added to the design rather than like a bias.
Measured, as the relative error of the worst design column per ladder level
at a fixed $\theta$ over $\eps = 0.05$ to $0.3$: slopes of $1.0$ in $\eps$
for white-noise probes ($0.84$--$1.12$ on a $3\times 10^5$-point disc,
$0.97$--$1.21$ on real windows) and $2.0$ for smooth probes ($1.7$--$2.2$).
At $\eps = 0.1$ the white-noise column error runs from 1.5\% at level 0 to
10\% at level 5, and the cross-validation score at a fixed $\theta$ moves by
about $8\times 10^{-3}$ on the largest windows. A dipole-corrected cell --
carry $d_C$ and add $\nabla\phi_i(x_C)\cdot d_C$ to the design -- would
restore second order; the numbers do not ask for it.

\section{The graded cell rule}
\label{sec:grading}

\paragraph{The rule.} Work in the whitened coordinates of the window frame,
$u = L_w^{-1}(x - \mu_w)$ (\S\ref{sec:frame}). Build a $2^N$-tree on the
bounding box of $\{u_j\}$, splitting at the midpoint of every axis; a node is
split while it holds more than one point and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{diag}(\text{box}) \;>\; \eps\, d(\text{box}), \qquad
d(\text{box}) = \operatorname{dist}\big(0, \text{box}\big),
\label{eq:grading}
\end{equation}
the distance from the origin to the box's nearest point, zero when the box
contains the origin. Every leaf is a cell. So a multi-point cell at distance
$r$ from the centre has size $h \le \eps r$, and cells shrink to single
points as they approach the centre.

\paragraph{Uniform in the width.} For a Gaussian $g(r) = e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$
the midpoint rule on a cell of size $h$ at distance $r$ errs by about
$h\,|g'(r)|$,
\begin{equation}
h\,\frac{r}{\sigma^2}\, e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}
 = \frac{h}{r}\; t\, e^{-t/2}\Big|_{t = r^2/\sigma^2}
 \;\le\; \frac{2}{e}\,\frac{h}{r} \;=\; 0.74\,\frac{h}{r},
\label{eq:gauss-bound}
\end{equation}
the maximum over $\sigma$ at fixed $r$ falling at $r = \sqrt 2\,\sigma$, where
the kernel is still $e^{-1}$ of its peak. With $h \le \eps r$ the error is at
most $0.74\,\eps$ of the peak for every width $\sigma$ simultaneously. The
rule needs no estimate of the kernel's width -- which is the point, since the
row whose window is far too wide is exactly the row whose prior width is
wrong.

\paragraph{The modes' smoothness.} A Laguerre--Gaussian mode of radial
degree $p$ and angular order $\ell$ is a polynomial of degree $2p + \ell$
times the Gaussian: its finest structure is at scale $\sigma/(p + \ell)$, it
lives near $r \sim \sigma$, and beyond that the Gaussian envelope takes over.
Three regions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The singleton near field}, $r \lesssim h/\eps$ on a mesh of
  spacing $h$. A box containing the centre has $d = 0$ and splits down to
  single points, and no two points merge closer than $h/\eps$, since a
  two-point cell has diagonal at least $h$. The fit sees every point.
\item \emph{The mode's own radius}, $r \sim \sigma$: cells of size
  $\eps\sigma$ against structure at scale $\sigma/(p + \ell)$. The midpoint
  rule on a smooth integrand errs by the square of the ratio, $(\eps\,(p +
  \ell))^2$; resolving the structure at all asks for
  \begin{equation}
  \eps\,(p + \ell)_{\max} \;\lesssim\; 0.5 .
  \label{eq:resolution}
  \end{equation}
  A shell ladder to level $L$ holds every mode with $2p + \ell \le L$, so
  $(p + \ell)_{\max} = L$: at the ladder's top level of 5, $\eps \le 0.1$.
\item \emph{Beyond}, $r \gg \sigma$: cells grow as $\eps r$ but the mode
  decays as a polynomial times $e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$, and
  \eqref{eq:gauss-bound} already covers it.
\end{itemize}
A coarser $\eps$ under-resolves the high modes; their aliased columns look
like noise to the cross-validation, so the ladder stops lower. That is the
conservative failure, and every measured casualty at $\eps \ge 0.15$ is of
this kind -- a ladder that stopped early, never a fit that wandered.

\paragraph{The count.} In 2D the dyadic annulus $[r, 2r]$ has area $3\pi
r^2$ and cells of size $\eps r$, so about $3\pi/\eps^2$ cells per annulus and
\begin{equation}
K_c \;\sim\; \frac{3\pi}{\eps^2}\,\log_2\frac{R}{h}
\label{eq:count}
\end{equation}
for a window of radius $R$ on a mesh of spacing $h$: logarithmic in the
window's size, which is what makes the fit's cost scale under mesh
refinement, and finite in 3D, where an octree gives about $4\pi/\eps^3$ per
shell. Two corrections matter at practical sizes. The near field is a
\emph{singleton core} of about $7\pi/\eps^2$ points -- nothing merges within
$h/\eps$ of the centre, and the dyadic quantization keeps most cells single
out to a few times that -- so the law needs a floor: at $\eps = 0.05$ the
core is nearly the whole count below $10^4$ points, and no window below about
$2{,}000$ points coarsens at all. Where the grading bites, the constant sits
above $3\pi$, because the rule bounds the box \emph{diagonal} and dyadic
halving leaves leaves between half and the full allowed size. Measured on
ball windows of $10^5$ grid points, $K_c$ over \eqref{eq:count} runs from
$0.76$ at $\eps = 0.05$ (floor still binding) to $1.7$ at $\eps = 0.3$; a
$10^5$-point window becomes $21{,}600$ cells at $\eps = 0.05$, $7{,}700$ at
$0.1$ and $4{,}000$ at $0.15$.

\section{Why the window frame}
\label{sec:frame}

The window is the ellipsoid $\{x : (x - \mu_w)^\top \Sigma_w^{-1} (x - \mu_w)
\le 1\}$ with $\Sigma_w = \tau_w^2\, \mathrm{cap}_a(\Sigma_{\mathrm{prior}})$,
where $\mathrm{cap}_a$ floors the minor axes so that the axis ratio is at
most $a$ (\code{window\_aspect\_cap}). It is already the object that encodes
how much of the prior's shape the library trusts, so the cells are built in
its whitened coordinates: with $\Sigma_w = L_w L_w^\top$,
\begin{equation}
u = L_w^{-1}(x - \mu_w),
\label{eq:frame}
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:grading} grades by $|u|$, the Mahalanobis distance of
$\Sigma_w$. A ball window, $a = 1$, makes $L_w$ a multiple of the identity
and the grading Euclidean. A user who lifts the cap and trusts an anisotropic
prior gets the grading in that prior's own metric for free: a kernel aligned
with the window is resolved isotropically in the window's coordinates, and a
kernel rotated against the window loses resolution along its major axis by
the window's aspect ratio -- the loss the fit already accepts through the
cap. One tree, no new notion of trust. The cells are axis-aligned boxes in
the Cholesky coordinates (parallelograms in physical space when $L_w$ has
off-diagonal entries); the grading is the metric of $\Sigma_w$ regardless.

\section{Protected positions}

Three kinds of position stay singleton cells, copied bit for bit: a node
holding one is split until that point is alone.

\paragraph{The spike.} Its column in \eqref{eq:design} is $B_q =
\sqrt{m_\rho}\, z_{\mathrm{spike}, q}$, with no mass in it: the whitened
extra is $\what e = \sqrt{m_\rho}\,\Mtwo^{-1/2} e$ and $e$ is one-hot, so the
$\Mtwo^{-1/2}$ cancels against the $\Mtwo^{1/2}$ in $\what z$. A singleton
reproduces the column exactly and keeps the meaning of $s$, of the spike's
measure and of the assembled diagonal entry; merged into a cell, the spike's
row would carry the cell's mass and $s$ would be silently rescaled.

\paragraph{The support of extra bases.} For a general extra column $E$ the
design column is the plain sum $\sqrt{m_\rho}\sum_j z_{jq} E_j$, without
masses, because an extra is a functional and not a kernel evaluation.
Aggregation preserves $\sum_j m_j z_j$, not $\sum_j z_j E_j$, so a general
$E$ is not coarsened consistently by \eqref{eq:aggregate}. What makes the
spike work generalizes with no special case: \code{coarsen\_window} takes a
set of \emph{protected positions} and keeps each a singleton;
\code{fit\_operator} passes the spike, a direct caller with extras passes the
union of their supports, and sparse extras are then exact. If the protected
set would exceed \code{coarsen\_above}, the row is left uncoarsened rather
than refused, and \code{fit\_points} says so.

\paragraph{The farthest point.} $R_w = \max_j |x_j - \mu_0|$
(\code{window\_radius}) is both the window-containment admissibility bound
on a released centre and the ladder's top rung. A centroid lies strictly
inside its cell's hull, so without protection $R_w$ would silently tighten;
the point farthest from the centre in physical distance stays a singleton
(ties to the smallest window position). The bottom rung,
\code{local\_spacing}, is preserved by the singleton near field of
\S\ref{sec:grading}. The spike and the farthest point are distinct unless
the window has one point, so the coarse set always has at least two.

\section{Deployment, and the full-window re-score}

The deployed row is the fitted kernel's $\tau$-ellipsoid intersected with the
full window,
\begin{equation}
\text{support} \;=\; \text{window} \;\cap\; \{\, \|u_\theta\| \le \tau \,\},
\label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
and nothing in the coarsening writes the window. So the guarantee is:
\emph{the deployed support is the fit window; the fit's quadrature on it is
coarsened with controlled error.} Near the centre the two quadratures agree
exactly, far out the kernel is small, and every deployed point lies in a cell
the fit saw -- the out-of-window extrapolation pathology that forced
``deployed support $=$ window'' cannot recur inside it.

What can go wrong is a score. On a coarsened row the cross-validation score
that drives the ladder and the baseline guard is a coarse-quadrature score,
and it is optimistic: the coarse quadrature adds noise to the design, and the
coarse baseline moves too. After the search, the two finalists -- the winner
and the baseline -- are re-scored on the full $x$, $m$, $z$ (one
\code{linear\_cv\_score} each, $O(Km)$ for $m$ modes, negligible against a
fit; the coefficients are not refit). \code{score} and
\code{baseline\_score} report those numbers, and the guard ``ship iff score
$<$ baseline'' is taken on them. Measured on the fifteen largest windows: a
model's coarse score differs from its full-window score by up to $0.018$ at
$\eps = 0.1$ and $0.056$ at $0.3$; the guard taken on coarse scores flips no
decision at $\eps \le 0.15$ and claims 2 false wins over the baseline at
$0.2$ and 4 at $0.3$, never the reverse. With the re-score the quadrature's
optimism can cost a row its fit, never ship a bad one.

\paragraph{Released centres.} The remaining gap is a candidate whose centre
is fitted and displaced by $\|\mu - \mu_0\|$ from the grading's centre: it
sits on cells of size $\eps\,\|\mu - \mu_0\|$, and a needle narrower than
those cells is under-resolved there -- its coarse columns are aliased, its
coefficients can inflate, and its coarse score says nothing about the fine
evaluation at deployment. Coarsened rows therefore arm the resolution rule
(\code{resolution\_eps}, set to \code{coarsen\_eps}): a released candidate is
admissible only if
\begin{equation}
\min \text{axis} \;\ge\; \eps\, \|\mu - \mu_0\|\, \max\big(1, (p + \ell)_{\max}\big),
\label{eq:released}
\end{equation}
the floor of 1 being the pure Gaussian, whose structure is at $\sigma$
itself. Pinned candidates are untouched, and the re-score is the consistency
check behind the rule: an aliasing artifact scores well on the cells and
badly on the points. Dead rows are safe by themselves -- zero data give zero
coefficients whatever the design.

\section{Determinism}

\code{coarsen\_window} is a pure function of its arguments: no threads, no
randomness, no dependence on allocation. The split is canonical (midpoints; a
point on a split plane goes to the upper child; empty children are dropped),
cells are emitted sorted by the smallest window position they contain, and
each cell accumulates \eqref{eq:aggregate} in ascending position. The same
inputs give bit-identical outputs, so the library's bit-identity across
thread counts and runs is untouched. In the distributed fit each rank's
window array is merged in ascending global id and is therefore independent
of the rank layout; a pure function of that array in that order is
rank-independent too, and the MPI gate with coarsening on passes bitwise at
1, 2 and 4 ranks ($564$ of $600$ rows coarsened, $20{,}305$ window points
fitted on $13{,}046$ cells). Setting \code{coarsen\_above = 0} restores the
pre-coarsening path byte-identically.

\section{Using it}

Two knobs on \code{OperatorFitConfig} and one diagnostic:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{coarsen\_above} (default $3{,}000$; $0$ switches it off):
  windows with more points than this are coarsened. A few thousand is the
  useful range -- below about $2{,}000$ points the grading is the identity and
  buys nothing. On by default since 2026-09-08, after field validation on a
  continental ice-sheet Hessian took the worst rank's fit from $2{,}015$\,s to
  $588$\,s at the same prior, with the operator, the QC ladder and the
  deployed solve unchanged. Fits on rows above the trigger are therefore not
  bitwise identical to releases before that date.
\item \code{coarsen\_eps} (default $0.1$): the grading ratio of
  \eqref{eq:grading}; \eqref{eq:resolution} at the ladder's top level of 5.
\item \code{FitDiagnostics::fit\_points}: per row, what the fit ran on --
  the window size, or the coarse count when the trigger fired. Whether the
  bound is binding is readable here and nowhere else
  (\code{DistFitResult::fit\_points\_total} sums it).
\end{itemize}
The $\eps$ sweep of \code{experiments/window-coarsening.md} is
Table~\ref{tab:sweep}: sixty real rows of the heat-equation inverse problem
at four mesh spacings, windows of $246$ to $99{,}856$ points, 60 probes, a
shell ladder to level 5 decided by cross-validation alone. Every coarse fit is re-scored on the full window.
``Fits'' is the fraction of rows whose fit beats the full-window baseline,
against $0.87$ for the full fits; ``same level'' the fraction shipping the
full fit's ladder level; ``max $\Delta$score'' the worst full-window score
change over the sixty rows; ``max coarse$-$full'' the largest gap
between a model's coarse-quadrature score and its full-window score on the
fifteen largest windows, and ``false wins'' how often the guard taken on
coarse scores would have shipped a model the full-window scores reject.

$\eps = 0.1$ keeps every guard decision and the ladder level on 97\% of
rows, the fitted axes within $10^{-4}$ in $\log_{10}$ and the orientation
within $0.25^\circ$, $c_0$ within $0.02\%$, for $9\times$ less wall time
overall and 11--54$\times$ on the $10^5$-point windows (23--30\,s to
0.5--1.4\,s). $\eps = 0.15$ loses the first fit to an early ladder stop;
$\eps \ge 0.2$ loses 10--17\% of the fits and makes the coarse score
dishonest. The motivating case, a prior too wide by decades, is the easy one:
the kernel then lies inside the singleton core and is fitted exactly (on the
rows with a $4\times$ widened prior the score is within $0.0003$ of the full
fit's at every $\eps$ up to $0.2$). The rows that feel $\eps$ are
honest-prior rows on fine meshes, whose kernels fill their windows.

Cost: a cell costs what a point costs ($5.7\times 10^{-6}$ against
$6.0\times 10^{-6}$\,s per candidate), so the saving is exactly the cell
ratio $K_c/K$; by \eqref{eq:count} a $10^5$-point row at $\eps = 0.1$ costs
less than a $10^4$-point row does today. The coarsening itself is
$O(K\log K)$ and is not the bottleneck.
